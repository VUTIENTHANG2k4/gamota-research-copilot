2026 Southeast Asia Mobile Game Marketing Report

2026 SEA mobile game report (SocialPeta & Gamota): downloads fell yet Vietnam revenue rose 7.5% to $177M; 46.2K advertisers, +19.7% YoY. RPG, SLG, Vietnam.

SocialPeta & GamotaSeptember 01, 2026

Downloads Decline, Revenue Grows 7.5% — Where’s Vietnam’s Next Growth Opportunity?

Southeast Asia has long been the “first stop” for Chinese mobile games going globally, thanks to its cultural proximity, relatively low user acquisition costs, and large user base. But in the first half of 2026, the market’s underlying dynamics are quietly shifting: downloads have generally declined year over year, while revenue has grown against the trend in several markets. As the growth dividend from new advertisers fades, refined operations among existing publishers and developers are becoming the key to winning. The old strategy of “scaling ad spend to drive growth” is losing effectiveness, while a new set of challenges—choosing the right markets, targeting the right genres, and getting localization right—now faces every company expanding globally.

SocialPeta and Gamota have jointly released the 2026 Southeast Asia Mobile Game Marketing Report. Drawing on SocialPeta’s advertising creative database of 1.7 billion+ ad creatives covering 70+ countries and regions and 80+ global advertising channels, combined with Gamota’s 13 years of localized publishing experience in Vietnam, the report provides a comprehensive analysis of growth opportunities in Southeast Asia’s mobile gaming market in 2026—from regional UA trends and an in-depth look at Vietnam, to leading advertiser case studies and winning creatives in the RPG and strategy genres.

The 44-page report covers the following key topics:

  • Download, revenue, and advertising trends across the Southeast Asian market
  • In-depth insights into Vietnam: policies, genres, leading titles, and market entry strategies
  • Marketing case studies of high-performing MMOs, Chinese casual games, and unconventional SLG advertisers
  • Advertising landscapes and winning creative analysis for two major genres: RPG and strategy games

Market Overview: Existing Advertisers Ramp Up Investment as Growth Dynamics Shift

From a market perspective, Southeast Asian countries show a clear divergence between “scale” and “value.” Indonesia leads in downloads but ranks relatively low in IAP revenue, while Thailand ranks first in IAP revenue. Malaysia and Singapore have smaller download bases, but higher levels of payer engagement. The report highlights that the key to market selection lies in product-market fit rather than simply comparing rankings: scale-driven products depend on broad user reach, while high-value mid-core and hardcore games benefit more from higher payer density. Meanwhile, Cambodia emerged as a “dark horse” in the first half of the year, with downloads up 11.8% YoY and IAP revenue up 28.5% YoY.

According to SocialPeta, the average number of mobile game advertisers actively running campaigns in Southeast Asia reached 46,200 in 2026, up 19.7% YoY and 5.2% MoM, indicating continued expansion in the region’s advertising market. However, the share of newly active advertisers averaged just 14.3% per month. This suggests that market growth is being driven primarily by increased investment from existing advertisers, while the barrier to entry for new players has risen significantly, intensifying competition.

On the creative side, Southeast Asia shows a distinct pattern compared with the global market: an average of 74.4% of advertisers continue to launch new creatives each month, yet new creatives account for only 40.3%, down 2.87% YoY. This indicates that advertisers in Southeast Asia still rely heavily on reusing existing creatives and extending proven concepts, reinforcing the idea that “one winning creative can run for the long term.”

In terms of creative formats, video accounts for 73.7% of all creatives, making it the dominant format for user acquisition in Southeast Asia. Among video ads, medium-length videos of 16–60 seconds account for more than 76%. This is driven in part by practical considerations: Indonesia and the Philippines have median mobile download speeds of just 29 Mbps and 36 Mbps, respectively, making medium-length videos a good balance between loading costs and gameplay storytelling. At the platform level, Android accounts for 83.3% of advertisers, while iOS creative investment for mid-core and hardcore products is approximately 2× that of casual products, indicating fiercer competition for high-value users on iOS.

Vietnam Deep Dive: Downloads Fall 9.7% — Why Is Revenue Up 7.5%?

As one of the top three Southeast Asian markets by downloads, accounting for 18.3% of the regional total, Vietnam is a key focus of this report. From a local market perspective, Gamota finds that Vietnam combines a proven user acquisition scale with significant monetization upside. Its share of IAP revenue (10.6%) is notably lower than its share of downloads, indicating substantial untapped value potential.

In the first half of 2026, mobile game downloads in Vietnam reached 76.5 million, down 9.7% YoY. However, the market began to recover in May and June, with June reaching 14.0 million downloads, the highest monthly figure in H1. Meanwhile, total revenue reached $177 million, up 7.5% YoY, with monthly revenue remaining relatively stable at $28.7–30.6 million. Revenue is growing faster than user acquisition, signaling that the market’s ability to convert users into value is strengthening.

Opportunities by genre are particularly clear: sports simulation downloads surged 242.2% YoY, shooting games grew 181.0%, and idle RPGs increased 21.4%—the only three growing subgenres among the Top 10. On the revenue side, shooting games surged 579.4% YoY, while idle RPG revenue grew 40.2%. Among leading titles, Free Fire MAX ranked No. 1 on both charts, generating 8.08 million downloads and $16.7 million in revenue.

The regulatory environment also warrants close attention. Resolution No. 80 elevated gaming to the status of a cultural industry, while Decrees No. 147/2024 and No. 174/2026 introduced stricter requirements covering licensing, player identity verification, minor protection, and data governance. Regulatory compliance has therefore become a prerequisite for entering the Vietnamese market.

The report also identifies two proven paths to success in Vietnam through two benchmark cases:

  • Value-driven path — Crossfire: Legends: In its first two months after launch, the game generated 2.39 million downloads and $3.58 million in revenue. Despite having a smaller user base, it significantly outperformed comparable tactical shooters with higher download volumes in terms of early revenue. Its combination of familiar tactical gameplay and tangible skin value helped rapidly convert demand into spending.
  • Scale-driven path — Play Together VNG: Nearly four years after launch, the game still ranks No. 5 in downloads and No. 2 in revenue, sustaining long-term user acquisition through a repeatable social system built around collection and progression, party-based gameplay, and UGC.

The common foundation behind both paths is local familiarity × repeatable engagement × tangible value × sustained operational execution.

Popular Advertisers: Three Southeast Asia Strategies

The report’s third section uses SocialPeta to break down three representative products and their approaches to the Southeast Asian market:

  • Chinese casual game expansion — Thế Giới Hoa Viên Của Tôi: The game has been advertised for 180+ days, with more than 15,000 creatives launched. High-performing creatives often open with chat-message-style scenes to lower users’ psychological barriers, then create a visual contrast between 2D Chinese ink-painting aesthetics and realistic flower bouquets. By emphasizing a sense of relaxation and emotional healing, the campaign precisely targets female casual players in Southeast Asia. One creative generated an estimated 390,000 impressions over 51 days.
  • Legacy IP MMO — Ragnarok: The New World: This 20-year-old classic IP has accumulated 51,000 creatives to date. In Indonesia, its ads use real-life streamers with highly emotional opening hooks and Indonesian-language commentary, effectively combining nostalgia with gameplay demonstrations. One creative generated an estimated 760,000 impressions in just two days.
  • Unconventional SLG — Lands of Jail: With 600+ days of advertising and 236,000 creatives to date, the game stands out in the global SLG market with its differentiated “prison-building” theme. Its bright, high-saturation visual style lowers the barrier to understanding the gameplay. One creative generated an estimated 1.59 million impressions over 38 days, with a heat score of 864.

Genre Insights: RPG’s “Dual-Track Landscape” and Strategy Games’ “Side-Mode Hooks”

The RPG genre shows a clear “dual-track landscape.” On one hand, Ragnarok: The New World dominates the RPG rankings in Indonesia, Thailand, and the Philippines, with 5,900–8,400 creative sets per market, accounting for 26%–40% of its global advertising output. On the other hand, Vietnam’s market is largely dominated by local publishers such as VPLAY and VNG, whose MMO titles focus on wuxia, xianxia, and Three Kingdoms themes. 82%–95% of their creatives are exclusively targeted at the domestic Vietnamese market, highlighting the strong localization of the local MMO ecosystem.

The strategy genre, meanwhile, is dominated by survival SLGs. Dark War Survival from Omnilojo produced 57,000 creatives over the past 90 days, ranking No. 1 in the strategy category in both Indonesia and the Philippines. Its advertising in a single market accounts for more than half of its global output at its peak. Kingshot has also expanded across multiple markets, including Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, and Singapore.

A clear trend toward “casualization” in strategy creatives is also emerging: tower defense elements and side-game mechanics are increasingly being used as the primary hooks for SLG user acquisition, making complex strategy gameplay more accessible to a broader audience.

At the channel level, Meta platforms carry approximately 93% of top-performing RPG creatives and 95% of top-performing strategy creatives. Video accounts for more than 80% of creatives in both genres, with an average duration of 39–43 seconds, while English-language copy remains the dominant format.

One particularly noteworthy finding is the “six-market hit rate” of top-performing creatives. Vietnam stands out with significantly lower rates—just 31% for RPG and 34% for strategy—further confirming that Vietnam has its own distinct local creative ecosystem, and directly reusing generic creatives across markets has limited effectiveness.

In 2026, the era of “blindly scaling user acquisition” in Southeast Asia’s mobile gaming market is over. The widening gap between downloads and revenue, intensifying competition among existing advertisers, and Vietnam’s stricter compliance and localization requirements are all pushing developers and publishers to shift from a “traffic mindset” to a “value mindset.” The priority is to first determine whether the goal is scale or value, then align the product, monetization strategy, and local execution around the same core player motivation.

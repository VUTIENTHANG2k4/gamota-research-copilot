## Market Overview

# CHAPTER 1 - Market Overview

The Vietnam Game Market operates through free-to-play mobile titles, PC games, premium releases, advertising-supported casual games, subscriptions, and esports-linked communities. An estimated **58.5 million active gamers in 2025** created a broad engagement base, while approximately 7% to 10% of mobile players made purchases. Revenue therefore depends on payer conversion, live operations, advertising yield, and sustained content updates.

Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi form the principal development, publishing, technology, and investment hubs. Vietnam's wider production ecosystem includes more than **3,000 game studios and development teams**, while a narrower commercial census identified approximately 210 actively operating studios in 2025. Concentrated access to engineering talent, payment partners, media buyers, and international publishers lowers coordination costs for scalable game operations.

Market access is shaped by Decree 147/2024/ND-CP, issued on November 9, 2024 and effective from December 25, 2024. The framework requires verified player registration, parental involvement for users below 16, controlled virtual-item use, and retention of specified user data for six months after account termination. Compliance capability directly affects launch timing, customer acquisition, platform integration, and operating costs.

Vietnam is transitioning from a primarily domestic publishing market into an export-oriented game-development center. Vietnamese-created games generated an estimated **10.3 billion downloads in 2025**, while local studios were projected to earn more than USD 430 Mn from global audiences. This strengthens Vietnam's position in casual, music, puzzle, simulation, and hybrid-monetization production, although global user-acquisition costs remain a key strategic constraint.

## Future Outlook

The Vietnam Game Market is projected to expand from USD 575 Mn in 2025 to approximately USD 997 Mn by 2031, representing a forecast CAGR of 9.6%. This compares with a historical CAGR of 6.3% during 2020-2025. Growth will be led by mobile live-service titles, stronger in-app purchase conversion, rewarded advertising, export-oriented studios, cloud delivery, and expansion of esports-linked engagement. The active gamer base is modeled to reach approximately 73.8 million by 2031, while paying-player penetration is expected to increase from 9.0% to 12.0%.

Future value growth is expected to outpace user growth as publishers improve monetization rather than relying solely on audience expansion. Mobile games are forecast to account for approximately 86% of domestic revenue by 2031, supported by broadband smartphone access and lower distribution barriers. Cloud gaming will remain a smaller revenue pool but should record the fastest platform growth as network performance improves. Investors should prioritize companies with durable intellectual property, measurable retention, disciplined user-acquisition economics, compliant identity systems, and the ability to publish across both domestic and international storefronts.

## Market Challenges

### Licensing and Identity Compliance

Decree 147 introduced mandatory identity and operating controls from **December 25, 2024 (Vietnam)**, increasing compliance requirements for publishers and platforms. 

* Player accounts require **full name, birth date, and Vietnamese mobile number (2024 regulation, Vietnam)**, creating verification, integration, and data-governance costs. 
* Users below **16 years of age (2024 regulation, Vietnam)** must register through a parent or guardian, affecting onboarding design and youth-focused monetization. 
* Specified player information must be retained for **six months after account termination (2024 regulation, Vietnam)**, increasing cybersecurity and data-storage obligations. 

### Low Paying Conversion and Revenue Concentration

Only approximately **7% to 10% of mobile players (2025, Vietnam)** make purchases, constraining monetization despite strong user engagement. 

* Higher-spending players represent only **2% to 3% of mobile users (2025, Vietnam)**, concentrating revenue and increasing exposure to churn among a small payer cohort. 
* The top 50 mobile titles generated approximately **USD 91.66 Mn (2025, Vietnam)**, demonstrating that revenue is concentrated in games with strong acquisition, retention, and live-operations capabilities. 
* A modeled **9.0% payer penetration (2025, Vietnam)** means small conversion errors materially affect forecasts, valuation, marketing budgets, and return on content investment. ([kenresearch.com](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/vietnam-video-game-market))

### Fragmented Production and Scale Economics

Vietnam combines more than **3,000 development teams (2025, Vietnam)** with a much smaller base of scaled commercial studios, creating uneven operating maturity. 

* A narrower industry assessment identified approximately **210 actively operating studios (2025, Vietnam)**, indicating that many teams remain small, project-based, or commercially unscaled. 
* Vietnamese studios launched approximately **27,388 new games (2025, Vietnam)**, increasing competition for visibility and reducing the probability that individual titles achieve durable economics. 
* New-title volume increased by approximately **13% in 2025 (Vietnam)**, intensifying user-acquisition competition and raising the value of differentiated intellectual property and proprietary distribution.

The highest-quality opportunities combine proprietary content, repeatable production systems, first-party player data, international storefront access, and measurable conversion from engagement into recurring revenue.

**Data used:** More than 10.3 billion projected downloads (2025); 18.4% modeled cloud-gaming CAGR through 2031.

**So what:** Capital should target studios with owned intellectual property and proven retention, not undifferentiated high-volume production.

#### Q: How does regulation affect game publishers entering Vietnam?

**A:** Publishers must plan for licensing, verified player registration, parental controls, virtual-item restrictions, data retention, and local operational accountability. Decree 147/2024/ND-CP became effective on December 25, 2024 and requires specified identity information, including a Vietnamese mobile number. Users below 16 require parent or guardian involvement. Enforcement risk is material because unauthorized game distribution can be restricted. Compliance should therefore be embedded into product architecture, launch sequencing, customer support, and data-governance budgets.

**Data used:** Decree effective December 25, 2024; six-month post-account data-retention requirement.

**So what:** International publishers need a licensed local operating model and technical compliance workstream before commercial launch.

#### Q: How attractive is Vietnam compared with neighboring game markets?

**A:** Vietnam is smaller by domestic revenue than Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, and Malaysia, but it offers the highest modeled forecast CAGR among the selected peers. Its advantages include 85.6 million internet users, an 84.2% penetration rate, strong mobile-network improvement, and a highly productive export-development ecosystem. Vietnam is therefore better positioned as a growth and production market than as the region's largest current consumer-revenue pool. Peer-country strategies should account for these different demand and supply characteristics.

**Data used:** Vietnam ranking fifth among selected peers (2025); 9.6% forecast CAGR (2026-2031).

**So what:** Regional strategies should use Vietnam for both domestic growth and globally distributed content development.

#### Q: What are the main risks to the market forecast?

**A:** The principal risks are weak payer conversion, rising user-acquisition costs, title concentration, licensing delays, data-compliance expenses, and fragmented studio capabilities. Only approximately 7% to 10% of mobile players make purchases, while higher-spending users represent a smaller 2% to 3% cohort. This creates earnings volatility when major titles lose engagement. Global storefront-policy changes and advertising-market fluctuations can also affect export-oriented studios. The constrained scenario assumes slower payer growth and continued pressure on acquisition economics.

**Data used:** 7% to 10% mobile payer range (2025); 2% to 3% higher-spending users (2025).

**So what:** Operators should diversify titles, channels, revenue models, and player cohorts while maintaining strict acquisition payback thresholds.
